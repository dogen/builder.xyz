## Life / interests deep dive

Meanwhile the local working tree is full of uncommitted changes to `quartz.config.yaml`, `custom.scss`, and `static-pages/`.

It's the homelab instinct applied to writing: build the rack, tune the config, never actually run the workload. Enjoyable and legible, but it defers the part that's exposing.

The staged-vs-live convention deserves a second look for the same reason. You chose it in July to keep drafts private, which was sensible. Two weeks on, it functions mostly as a place things go to not be published — every entry you've made is still sitting behind a comment. Worth asking whether it's protecting you from a bad draft or from publishing at all.

What genuinely has momentum: **Btrail.** It's the one thing with real-world artifacts behind it. It's also the thing most fenced off from this side of the site. Fine as a boundary — but [[projects]] saying nothing at all reads less like a boundary and more like neglect.

What's being neglected: the physical, tactile obsessions. The XR200 on the porch is the most alive detail in the entire vault and exists in exactly one sentence. Same for adobe and rammed earth — a stated obsession with zero notes, zero links, zero sources.

## Workflows to build

- **Unstage-on-Friday.** *Trigger:* weekly, same slot as this review. *Action:* open each section file, move one staged entry out of its comment block. *Payoff:* forces the queue to drain and makes "staged" a waypoint rather than a destination.
- **A comment-syntax lint in CI.** *Trigger:* every push. *Action:* grep `content/` for `<!—`, `<!–`, and `] (` — the em-dash comment opener and the broken-link pattern. *Payoff:* catches exactly the two bugs already live in the vault, and every future paste from a smart-quotes editor.
- **Build-log capture for the XR200.** *Trigger:* whenever you have the bike apart. *Action:* one line and one phone photo into `notes.md` — what came off, what the measurement was, what surprised you. *Payoff:* a torn-down top end is unrecoverable from memory three weeks later, and this is the highest-signal, lowest-effort content you have access to.
- **Commit the working tree.** *Trigger:* now. *Action:* the SCSS and config changes have been uncommitted since early July. *Payoff:* the deployed site doesn't reflect your local work, and an uncommitted tree is why a "did it publish?" question is hard to answer.
- **Date-stamp entries on capture.** *Trigger:* every new entry. *Action:* append `— added YYYY-MM-DD`. *Payoff:* makes staleness visible, which is the precondition for any review like this being useful next quarter.
